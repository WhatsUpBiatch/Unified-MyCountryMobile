US tax and compliance — what to buy, what to build, what to skip

4 September 2026 · vendor research with real published prices where they exist, checked against what our platform actually has today.

Read this first — it changes the tax question

The tax service we already use cannot do telephone tax. Its product tax-code list has no code for telecommunications, VoIP, telephony, or mobile service, and nothing that computes E911, universal service, or state telecom surcharges. It taxes software subscriptions. We sell phone service.

So even after the pre-tax bug is fixed and registration is done, what we would collect is general sales tax on a service — not communications tax. That is the gap, and no amount of configuring will close it.

And the good news — you do not need to buy one yet

22 of our 23 customers are outside the US (India, Colombia, Nigeria, Mexico). One is US. Our US telecom revenue is effectively zero. The federal threshold below which a provider does not pay universal service contributions at all is $37,175 of combined interstate and international US telecom revenue for 2026. We are nowhere near it.

A communications tax engine is a five-figure annual commitment. Buying one now would be the most expensive line item on the platform, protecting revenue that does not exist. Build the shape now, buy the engine at a defined trigger.

Part A — US taxation

A1 · Three different things, often confused

LayerWhat it isWho solves it
1. Sales & use taxOrdinary state sales tax on the subscription, by the customer's billing address.Our current tax service does this. Every general engine does.
2. Communications tax and surchargesState and local telecom taxes, state universal service, 911/E911 fees per line, franchise fees, gross receipts, right-of-way. Different rates, different jurisdictions, different rules from sales tax — and rooftop-level, not ZIP-level.Only a communications tax engine. This is the gap.
3. Federal regulatory contributionsFCC Form 499-A / 499-Q, federal universal service, telecom relay service, number administration, local number portability. Plus state utility registrations and the robocall mitigation database.Not an API at all. These are filings. They need a regulatory filing agent or a communications law firm.

An engine gives you layer 1 and 2. It does not file your 499. People buy the engine, assume they are covered, and are not.

A3 · Recommendation

Now — free, this month

  1. Fix the pre-tax bug (item M3 in the billing audit). The tax figure is calculated correctly and then discarded in favour of the pre-tax total. One line. Nothing else in this section matters until it is done.
  2. Decide which entity sells to whom. If the selling company is Indian and the customers are Indian, GST is the real obligation and our current tax service handles India. That is a question only you can answer, and it changes everything below it.
  3. Build the invoice shape now. Add a tax-and-fee lines table to the bill — one row per charge with a code, a description, a jurisdiction, a base and an amount — behind a small calculator interface with exactly two methods. Today it is backed by our current service. Later it is backed by an engine, and that is a config change, not a rewrite. This is a day of work and it is the cheapest insurance on this page.
  4. Do not add the regulatory fees by hand. Inventing an "E911 recovery fee" without a registration behind it is worse than charging nothing.

The trigger to buy — whichever comes first

  • US end-user telecom revenue reaches a $30,000 a year run rate (below the $37,175 federal threshold, so you are ready before you cross it), or
  • you register as a US interconnected VoIP provider, or
  • you sell a US customer a US number with a US service address — a single one of these creates a real state obligation.

At the trigger: get quotes from taxTheta and CereTax + TaxConnex and Avalara for Communications, in that order of likely value for money at our size.

A4 · How to run the quotes so the numbers are comparable

Send all three the same figures — monthly invoice count, monthly line count, US states with customers, annual US revenue — and ask these five questions in writing. The answers, not the headline price, are where the cost hides:

  1. Is the meter per invoice or per line item? A ten-line invoice can cost ten times what you assumed.
  2. What is the annual minimum commitment and the one-off implementation fee?
  3. Is filing and remittance included, or is that a separate contract?
  4. Is the rate lookup rooftop-level or ZIP-level? ZIP-level gets telecom tax wrong, and the error is yours to pay.
  5. Do they handle FCC Form 499 and state registrations, or only calculation?

Part B — Compliance

Your fraud-controls document is the right frame, so this section is scored against it directly rather than against a competitor's help pages. Two things stand out.

The expensive-looking part is free, and the free-looking part is where we are exposed

The entire signup-screening stack in Stage 2 costs roughly $0 to $30 a month at our volume. It is nearly all free tiers and open data.

Stage 1 — the four controls that actually cap a loss — costs nothing at all in vendor fees, because it is entirely our own code. And we have almost none of it. That is the real finding.

B3 · The order to do it in

  1. Spend cap and destination default-deny — Stage 1.1 and 1.2, our own code, no vendor, no budget. Your document is right that these come first: they work even when screening misses, and screening always misses something. They are also blocked on the same missing piece as the billing work — nothing debits a balance for usage — so do them together with the metering project in the billing audit.
  2. Fix the 10DLC webhook host — one line, production is pointing at a QA machine.
  3. Extend Turnstile to signup and SSO — the signup form is the one that matters most and is the one still unchecked.
  4. Velocity limits and the outbound gate — Stage 1.3 and 1.4.
  5. The five signals and the scorer — Stage 2. Roughly $4 a month. Start from the reference implementation in the fraud-shield skill rather than writing it fresh.
  6. E911 address capture and validation at Gate 3 — free on the USPS API, and it is a federal obligation as well as a fraud control.
  7. Traffic-health metrics per customer — the call records already hold what is needed; this is a query and a dashboard.
  8. Traceback owner, number inventory export, porting readiness — Stage 5. Nearly free, and they are the only things that help if we are the ones cut off.
