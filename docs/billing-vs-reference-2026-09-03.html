Billing, Measured Against the Reference

Every billing screen read side by side with the reference product's own documentation, then checked against what the backend actually records. What works, what only looks like it works, and how much is left to build.

Checked 3 September 2026 Reference 36 help-centre articles, mirrored locally Our side 7,844 lines of screen code, the API, the live database

The screens are ahead of the system underneath them

This is the best-built part of the product. The ten screens are thoughtful, the money logic lives in tested modules, and where a figure has no source the screen says "Not available yet" rather than printing a zero. Against the reference, the shape is right: summary, usage, plan, licences, credit, invoices, cost centres — all present, all in the right order.

What is missing is nearly all beneath the screens. Nothing meters voice or text usage back to the plan. Usage never reaches the ledger. Tax has never been charged on any invoice. No PDF exists. No change log exists. And two things found on the way should be fixed today: the Ultimate plan is live at $2 a seat with fourteen companies on it, and the plan comparison table tells customers it costs $42.

Roughly six to eight engineering weeks of backend work closes the gap to the reference. The screens need perhaps one week of that, mostly wiring.

What the reference actually does

Seven rules their billing follows

Mined from the articles, not from screenshots. Each one decides a question their documentation never states outright.

1. A licence is a thing you buy, not a thing a user owns

"Dialpad charges for all purchased licenses, even if they're inactive and unassigned." Deleting a user frees the seat and keeps the charge. Removal is a separate, scheduled act at cycle end, reversible until then.

manage-dialpad-license-billing · dialpad-billing-faqs

2. Money is drawn in a fixed order, and the customer can see the order

Plan allowance first, then the matching commitment, then universal credits. Every usage record shows which wallet it came from.

dialpad-credits-and-usage · unified-credit-pool

3. Every charge is a record the customer can find

Usage history shows who, when, which number, type, duration, wallet source, cost and rate — per call, per text segment, per fax page. Filterable, exportable.

dialpad-credits-and-usage · credits-analytics

4. Tax is itemised, and it is regulatory, not just sales tax

USF, cost-recovery, E911 per line, state and local levies, GST/HST for Canada — each named on the bill, each based on the billing address.

view-your-dialpad-billing-summary

5. Failure is a state machine with a grace period

On hold: lines stay active, admin is emailed, banner shown, IVR reminder. Suspended: lines off, callers hear a message. Recovery is self-serve for the first, support for the second.

on-hold-suspended-accounts

6. Cost centres are metadata and never touch the invoice

A directory of codes; allocations at office, user or licence level that must total 100%; precedence user → licence → office; archive, never delete; CSV export for the ledger.

cost-centers

7. Every change to what you pay leaves a trace

A billing change log of licence adds and removals, filterable by user and month, exportable. Refunds have a rule: three days, no exceptions.

view-your-dialpad-billing-summary · cancellation-and-refund-faqs

Found on the way

Five things to fix before anyone is charged

TodayThe Ultimate plan is live at $2 a seat

The plan record says $2 a month. Fourteen companies are on it. It was charged at $2 on 27 and 29 August, at $42 once on 1 September, and at $2 again on 2 and 3 September. The plan comparison table — which reads a hardcoded file, not the plan record — tells every customer it costs $42.

So a customer sees $42, clicks through, and pays $2. Or the reverse, on the day somebody sets it back. A price edit leaves no trace: there is no audit of plan changes.

plans.Ultimate  original_price = 2   updated 2026-09-01 07:57   14 companies
src/lib/plan-catalogue.ts   monthlyPerSeat: 42
Completed plan payments, per seat:  27 Aug $2 · 29 Aug $2 · 1 Sep $42 · 2 Sep $2 · 3 Sep $2

Decision needed: is $2 a test price that was never put back, or is $42 the aspiration? Whichever it is, one of the two sources has to go. The plan record is what checkout charges, so it must be the truth and the catalogue must read from it — or seed it and never be read again.

ComplianceTax has never been charged on any invoice

Stripe Tax is wired correctly and runs on every plan purchase. It has returned zero every single time — 143 invoices, none with tax. Every billing address on file is in Mumbai, and the Stripe account is evidently registered for tax in no jurisdiction that matches.

The reference itemises USF, cost recovery, E911 per line, and state and local levies for US customers, and GST/HST for Canada. Whether your customers are in India or the US, the answer is the same: zero is wrong, and it is the kind of wrong that arrives as a letter.

SELECT COUNT(*), SUM(tax_amount > 0) FROM billings   →   143, 0
tax_location on every completed invoice:  Mumbai, MH, IN

MoneyUsage never reaches the ledger

When a text or fax is sent, the balance goes down and nothing is written. No invoice line, no statement row, no usage record. The Invoices and Statement screens are therefore true about plans and top-ups and blind to consumption. Voice does not even reduce the balance — that was the first report.

CompanyUsageBillingHelper.reserve()  →  company.update({credits, amount})   and nothing else
billings.type values ever written:  Plan Payment · DID Charges · Account Refill · Trial Plan ·
                                    License Purchase · DID Purchase · Add Member · DID Renew
# "Balance Deduct" exists in the enum. It has never been used.

This weekAuto-recharge is per person, and unprotected

The setting lives on the user, not the company. Two admins who both switch it on will both trigger a refill against the same shared balance. And the charge it places carries no idempotency key — every other recurring charge in the system has one. The two-minute cron has an overlap guard, so this is a duplicate-by-design risk rather than a race, but it is still two charges for one shortfall.

User.findAll({ attributes: ["auto_recharge_setting", …] })   ← per user
stripe.paymentIntents.create({ amount: refill_amount * 100 … })  ← no idempotencyKey

This weekTwo balances, two names, two screens

A company has credits (the plan's free credit, $20 on signup) and amount (money they topped up). Summary shows the first as "Credit balance". Statement shows the second as "Wallet balance". The code draws from credits first, then the wallet — exactly the reference's order — but tells the customer none of this. One card, both figures, and the sentence "we use your plan credit first, then your wallet" would fix it.

How much is left

The pending work, sized

Seventeen pieces. Grouped by why, ordered within each group by what unblocks the most. Estimates are engineering days for one person who knows the codebase.

Before charging anyone real money

  1. One price source

    Make the plan record the only truth. The comparison table, the usage rates and the add-on prices all read from the API. Delete the numbers from plan-catalogue.ts and addons.ts once it is done. Fix Ultimate first.

    1 day

  2. Rate voice calls and post them to the ledger

    From the first report. The rate table is complete; the lookup is missing. Write a Balance Deduct row per call.

    2–3 days

  3. Post SMS and fax to the ledger too

    The deduction helper already knows the amount and which balance it came from. Add one Billing.create inside its transaction.

    1 day

  4. Register for tax where the customers are

    Stripe Tax registration for the jurisdictions you sell into, and a decision on regulatory fees (E911, USF, cost recovery) if any customers are in the US. Mostly configuration and a conversation with an accountant.

    1–2 days + advice

  5. Auto-recharge at company level, with an idempotency key

    Move the setting to the company record, key the charge on company + day, and make the screen say whose card will be used.

    1 day

  6. Write the usage counters

    call_duration_used from the call ingester, a real sms_used column incremented by the SMS service, and toll_free_duration_used. Reset them on renewal. Three screens light up.

    1 day

To match the reference

  1. A usage-history endpoint

    Per record: who, when, number, type, duration, wallet source, cost, rate. Filter by person, month, service. Voice from the call table, SMS and fax from the new ledger rows. Then wire the Usage screen to it.

    3 days

  2. A billing change log

    Every seat add, seat removal, plan change, price edit and card change, with who did it. Twenty-two write sites today, none logged. Show it on Summary, export it.

    2 days

  3. A PDF invoice

    A document service that renders the same figures the screen shows, with the company's details and yours, numbered and dated. The screen already says this is missing.

    2–3 days

  4. On hold before suspended

    Today an expired plan deactivates every user on the next hourly cron. The reference gives a grace period with lines active, an email, a banner and an IVR reminder — then suspends. Add the state, the emails, and stop the nightly retry after a few attempts.

    2–3 days

  5. Refunds

    A three-day self-serve rule and an admin path. refund_amount exists on the ledger and is never written.

    1–2 days

  6. Grant the numbers a plan includes

    did_count is on every plan and read by nothing. Either grant them at signup or remove the promise from the comparison table.

    1 day

  7. Add-on catalogue and purchase

    An endpoint that lists add-ons with server-held prices, and a purchase path that prices on the server. Then a buy button. Decide first which of the eleven are genuinely for sale.

    2–3 days

  8. Cost-centre allocations and a report

    Attach centres to people, seats and locations at a percentage; apply the precedence rule the screen already documents; a spend-by-centre report with CSV. Needs the usage records first.

    3–5 days

  9. Invoice notes

    One text field on the ledger row, shown at the foot of the invoice.

    ½ day

Screen wiring, once the above exists

  1. One wallet card

    Both balances, the application order in a sentence, and the same label on every screen.

    ½ day

  2. Usage and Statement read real data

    Replace the "not metered yet" notes as each counter and the usage endpoint land; add person and service filters; use the rate table for the rate column.

    2 days

Total: roughly 28 to 36 engineering days — six to eight weeks for one person, half that for two. The first six items are the ones that stop you charging a customer wrongly; they are about a week and a half together. Nothing in this list is a rewrite. The screens stay; the system underneath them is finished.

Things I did not copy, on purpose

  • Licence pools across offices. The reference bills per office and pools credit between them. This product has one balance per company and no office-level billing. Adopting their model now would add cost for no customer who needs it. Decide only when a multi-location customer asks.
  • Flex versus contracted licences. Every seat here is month-to-month and self-serve. That is simpler than the reference and fine for launch.
  • Their prices. Their per-seat and per-minute figures are evidence of what the market accepts, not numbers to copy. Where this document names a price it is yours, from your own plan record.
  • Multi-currency. Everything here is USD. Their currency-partitioned pools are a problem you do not have yet.
